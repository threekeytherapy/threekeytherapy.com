Today I support employees who are running on empty, and with managers who carry other people's stress on top of their own.

## Track 1: Burnout recovery

**For employees showing signs of exhaustion, or already on the edge.** A structured programme of 7 to 9 sessions in three phases.

### Phase 0: Intake (1 session)

We map what is going on: symptoms, work situation, sleep, history, what has been tried. We agree what you want from the programme and whether it is the right fit. If it isn't, I'll say so and point you elsewhere.

### Phase 1: Out of survival mode (3 sessions)

Chronic stress keeps the body in a constant state of alert: shallow breathing, tight muscles, poor sleep, no real rest. These sessions use touch, breathing and conversation to settle the nervous system and create the first real sense of being able to stop.

You should expect to notice: deeper breathing, better sleep, less constant tension, more room to think.

### Phase 2: Boundaries, self-regulation and body signals (3 to 5 sessions)

Once the body has calmed down, we work on keeping it that way:

- **Recognising your body's signals** early, before they become a crisis (jaw, stomach, breath, fatigue, irritability)
- **Self-regulation:** practical tools you can use at your desk, in a meeting, or before a difficult conversation
- **Setting boundaries:** noticing where you say yes when your body says no, and practising the alternative

The number of sessions in this phase depends on you, and we decide together after phase 1.

## Track 2: Personal leadership at work

**For managers and team leads.** Leading people is an emotional job, and most of us were never taught how to do it without burning ourselves out.

This track works on four things:

1. **Your stress response under pressure.** What happens in your body when a deadline slips, a decision is contested or a team member is struggling, and how to stay clear-headed in it.
2. **The emotional load of managing people.** Carrying other people's problems, delivering bad news, holding the line without hardening.
3. **Conflict.** Staying regulated and present in difficult conversations instead of avoiding, escalating or freezing.
4. **Preventing stress and burnout, in yourself and in your team.** Recognising early signals, and building habits that keep you sustainable.

I've been on both sides of this: leading teams in a complex public-sector environment, and then retraining as a therapist. The track is practical, confidential and built around your actual situation, not a generic framework.

## Confidentiality

What is said and done in a session stays between the employee and me. Your organisation does not receive session content, diagnoses, or my notes, ever. The only information that reaches you is what the employee has explicitly agreed to share, normally limited to attendance.

Sessions are held at my practice, not on your premises, so that people can speak freely.

## Who this is not for

This is preventive and complementary support. It does not replace medical or psychological care, and it does not replace the occupational health route (bedrijfsarts / arbeidsarts) for employees on sick leave.

I recommend a GP or psychologist first, or alongside, for anyone experiencing severe depression, suicidal thoughts, or acute crisis. If I think someone needs that, I'll tell them.

## Questions

**How is this different from coaching or a standard burnout programme?**  

Coaching and talk therapy work mostly through conversation. I work through the body and nervous system, and use conversation alongside. Many people who have already tried talking about their stress find this the missing piece.

**Does it involve touch?**  

Yes, most sessions include massage-style bodywork on a treatment table. Clothing can be kept on, and each person decides what they are comfortable with. Employees are never required to take part in anything they don't want to.

**Can it replace a company's occupational health or reintegration process?**  

No. It runs alongside it.

**Can you work with a whole team?**  

Yes. Ask about team sessions in the scoping call.
